3. Prior Authorizations and AI

November 23, 2026

Can AI be used in the workflow process for Prior Authorizations (PA) in US Healthcare?

PAs are requirements from insurance plans that a provider get approval before a patient receives a medication, test, or service.

PAs have been disliked in surveys of doctors, for they are an administrative burden on over-digitized medical professionals.

Insurers argue that PAs are a check on spending, especially on certain wasteful procedures, and argue they establish necessity of care.

Enter WISeR, a CMS program designed to pilot AI evaluating PAs, and there are six vendors, one per state, running pilots of this now. While the data is still forthcoming, there was a FOIA request done by the Electronic Frontier Foundation and data came out in Sept and Oct 2026. This is worth examination because vendors running these AI tools gain compensation based on a share of averted expenditures, while clinicians sign off on denials. What we know so far from the launch weeks: differences by vendor/state, as Washington denied 53% while under 20% were denied in Arizona. Further, 99% of Arizona direct requests were done by mail, and over 90% of Washington's were done by fax. Fascinating, AI reviewing mail and fax in 2026!

These are early days for a CMS trial, and so this limited data release lacks full data on official approval, denial, appeal-overturn by vendor, state, service, and month. Democratic lawmakers are asking for turnaround times, denial rates and reasons, peer-to-peer reviews, and more. Late in 2026, we should receive CMS's snapshot of these pilots, and we'll gain more information on this cutting-edge use of AI to add PA to Traditional Medicare and potentially save costs. Whether or not this saves money, we should study patient outcomes and continue to survey doctors to broaden our understanding of AI in Healthcare in 2026 and beyond.
