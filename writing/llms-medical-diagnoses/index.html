1. LLMs and Medical Diagnoses

November 17, 2026

Do humans diagnose illnesses better with LLMs? I present here two different takes on the question.

https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-02545-1

A fascinating study came out in March 2026, with an RCT of 70 internal medicine specialists. The research compares using an AI system as first or second opinion vs using "Conventional Resources" like Google or PubMed. Overall, they found that AI may helped in diagnostics slightly, and that Conventional Resources scored slightly lower than AI alone or AI+human collaborations.

To me, this research highlights the collaboration of AI and Human Clinicians. However, I do want to circle back to those Conventional Resources like PubMed and Google Search as comparators. This research study may be highlighting some of the UI/UX issues and lesser capabilities of those older "conventional" resources. Much has been made elsewhere of the story of internet search, ads, and how quality often suffers over time in these tools. Side note: will AI quality hold up long-term better than Google search?

In any case, there has been an opening for better Search to gain huge market share as simple fact-finders. Models may also provide reasoning and deal with complex interactions of multiple variables. A skillful AI user will manipulate model choice and effort-selector on a case-by-case basis, offering a better Google or a more advanced analytic workflow.

One interesting aspect of the study was its survey of clinicians, who found they are more likely to agree with the statement "I am open to using AI to help with complex clinical reasoning". Thus, LLMs are being considered in the clinic today and may be tried in clinics in the near future.

But wait! There's more. A second article appears.

https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-025-04074-y

This other study of laypeople showed that LLMs may not have been better than conventional resources at aiding with diagnostics. I will note this was laypeople and they used ChatGPT 4o which is now a somewhat older AI model, and is markedly less powerful than November 2026 state of the art models. Whether or not the moving target of AI performance in real time actually stayed the same throughout trials, and whether these configurable AI services were used adeptly, there is certainly disagreement in medicine about the potential future for LLMs assisting in medicine.

Further study is needed soon, since there are medical, ethical, and yes financial incentives for it to be useful in improving healthcare outcomes. I expect to hear much more about AI in healthcare settings over the coming years. For now, it may be wise to raise awareness about LLMs and other kinds of statistical model, and perhaps provide training in schools about these models.
