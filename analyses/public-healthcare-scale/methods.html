Methods and sources

This analysis compares annual CMS program spending with annual nominal GDP and converts spending to 2024 dollars using BEA’s GDP implicit price deflator. Source rows and extracted values are retained in the downloadable CSV; the automated validation report is included with the project data.

Definitions and selected rows

  • Medicare: CMS NHEA total program spending, including benefits and associated government administration and non-medical expenditures for private plans. The intended all-program row is row 7 of NHE2024.csv (“Medicare”), not Medicare entries repeated beneath a service category.
  • Medicaid: CMS NHEA “Medicaid (Title XIX)” total, federal and state/local. CMS maps Form CMS-64 spending to NHEA categories, adjusts timing and categories, removes Medicare premium buy-ins to avoid double counting, and includes administration and managed-care non-medical costs. Row 8 is the complete program total. CHIP (Title XIX and XXI) appears separately on row 11 and is excluded.
  • VA healthcare: CMS NHEA “Department of Veterans Affairs” total, row 15. CMS methods describe detailed VA quarterly expenditures crosswalked to NHE service categories and converted to calendar-year totals, with validation against Treasury and VA budget data. The defined program covers VHA health care and associated administration. It is not the full VA budget. Personal health care plus applicable health-program administration/non-medical costs are included; NHEA research and structures/equipment investment are separate categories. This series includes the VA direct and purchased care represented in the underlying VA expenditure data.

Years, units, and transformations

The common complete actual series is 1966–2024, the span for which all three selected CMS program rows have values. The visual focuses on 1995, 2005, 2015, and 2024, and growth compares 1995 with 2024. CMS detailed expenditure data are in millions of dollars and are divided by 1,000 to produce billions.

BEA NIPA Table 1.1.5, line 1, annual sheet T10105-A, supplies annual GDP in millions of current dollars. The workbook explicitly distinguishes it from quarterly sheet T10105-Q. GDP is divided by 1,000 to billions. BEA Table 1.1.9, line 1, annual sheet T10109-A, supplies the annual GDP implicit price deflator (index, 2017=100). The 2024 deflator is 125.587. The BEA Section 1 workbook states it was published October 29, 2026.

For each program and year: real spending = nominal spending × (2024 deflator ÷ year t deflator). For each year: GDP share = 100 × nominal program spending ÷ nominal GDP. Real growth is the change from 1995 real spending to 2024 real spending. Nominal growth is shown separately; subtracting the two growth rates does not identify a causal inflation contribution.

Checks and benchmark comparisons

  • Selected row labels and row positions are checked exactly; duplicate program names below service-category headings are not used.
  • All three 2024 program totals match CMS Table 19 at unrounded million-dollar precision. Medicare and Medicaid 2005, 2015, and 2024 totals match CMS Table 3 within its displayed $0.1 billion rounding. Table 3 has no 1995 column; 1995 values are taken from the full CMS annual historical detail series.
  • CMS detail total NHE ($5,278.588 billion) agrees with CMS summary ($5,278.60 billion) within rounding.
  • CMS summary GDP is $29,298 billion in 2024. BEA’s later vintage is $29,380.991 billion, a 0.28% difference. This is recorded as a revision-vintage difference rather than forced into a mixed source calculation.
  • The annual observations are contiguous and unique. The 2024 constant-dollar value equals the nominal value by construction; GDP shares are recomputed from nominal spending and BEA nominal GDP.

Comparability limits

The CMS source is its 2024 comprehensive-revision vintage, which revises the historical series with updated source data, benchmarks, and methods. BEA NIPA values are from a later release published October 29, 2026. These vintages need not match CMS’s embedded GDP values exactly. Medicare and Medicaid are financing programs; VA is also a direct care delivery system. The programs cover different populations and benefit packages. Spending per dollar of GDP describes national economic scale, not equivalent coverage or efficiency. GDP deflation adjusts for broad economy-wide price movement and cannot separate medical prices, service volume, population growth, or program eligibility changes.
